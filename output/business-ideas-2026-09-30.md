### 1. The RAM Harvest — Pull Memory Out of Dead Machines While DRAM Trades at 7x

> **Every broken laptop on Facebook Marketplace is now a $150 memory module wrapped in $40 of scrap — and almost nobody selling them knows it yet.**

**Source:** https://tech-insider.org/memory-chip-shortage-2026-ai-consumer-electronics/ — Tech Insider. DRAM spot prices are up nearly 700% year-over-year as data centers absorb ~70% of global memory production for AI.

Memory is the one PC component whose price has genuinely decoupled from reality. A 32GB DDR5 desktop kit that cost $80–$120 in mid-2025 now starts around $350 and is frequently out of stock; DDR5 modules that listed at $149 now list at $239 and up. Meanwhile the *used* market hasn't repriced nearly as fast — people selling a dead laptop or an old tower on Marketplace, Craigslist, or at an e-waste drop-off are still pricing the whole unit, not the RAM inside it. The arbitrage is buying broken or obsolete machines for scrap-adjacent prices, pulling the memory (a two-minute job with one screwdriver), testing it, and selling modules individually. Refurbished DDR4 ECC server memory is the same trade one tier up: enterprises are dumping high-capacity DDR4 as they move to DDR5, and DDR4 has risen far less than DDR5 from a much lower per-gigabyte base.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $400–$1,200 (inventory buys, MemTest USB stick, anti-static mat, screwdriver kit, label printer, padded mailers)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 1–2 weeks (first modules listed and sold)
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 6–10 hrs/week — sourcing on weeknights, teardown and listing on weekends
- 📈 **Return potential:** 3–8x on sourcing cost per module while the shortage holds; a $60 broken laptop routinely carries $80–$200 of memory at current prices
- 🔁 **Model:** Arbitrage / flip, with a recurring supply relationship once you have sources
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Basic teardown skill (YouTube-level), an eBay or Facebook Marketplace seller account, MemTest86 for verification
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** **Test every module and disclose that it is used/pulled** — there are documented cases of desoldered server chips being passed off inside "new" consumer kits, and misrepresenting pulls will get you banned and is fraud. Prices are volatile in both directions: SK Hynix says the shortage may persist past 2030, but Intel and several analysts point to 2027–2028 relief, so don't sit on inventory.
- 📦 **Realistic first batch:** 5–10 machines, 10–25 modules
- ⚡ **Speed to cash:** Fast — memory is a commodity with instant liquidity on eBay

**First steps**
1. Learn the identifiers this weekend: DDR4 vs DDR5, DIMM vs SODIMM, capacity, speed, and ECC/RDIMM vs UDIMM. Pull up sold listings on eBay for each and build a one-page pricing cheat sheet so you can value a machine on sight.
2. Set standing Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist alerts for "broken laptop," "for parts," "dead PC," "office cleanout." Cap your first offers at $40–$80 per machine and buy 5.
3. Tear down, pull the RAM, and run MemTest86 on each module. Anything with errors gets recycled, not sold — your feedback rating is the whole business.
4. List each module individually with clear "used, pulled, tested" wording and a photo of the MemTest pass screen. Price 10–20% under the cheapest comparable to move fast; you are competing on trust, not price ceilings.
5. Once you've done a dozen, go upstream: call two local IT asset disposition (ITAD) firms and e-waste recyclers and ask what they charge for a pallet of decommissioned DDR4 server memory sorted by part number. That's where the margin scales.
6. Reinvest into the next batch. Set a hard rule to sell within 30 days of acquisition so you're never holding a depreciating position if prices break.

### 2. Right-to-Repair Arrives in Texas — Be the Weekend Repair Bench That Was Illegal Last Year

> **Texas's consumer-electronics repair law took effect this month, which means the parts and manuals you couldn't legally get in January are now a mandated phone call away.**

**Source:** https://www.wastedive.com/news/right-to-repair-bills-2026-electronics-automotbiles-wheelchairs/809993/ — Waste Dive. Colorado and Washington's consumer-electronics repair laws took effect Jan. 1, 2026; Connecticut's provisions in July; **Texas's in September 2026**; 33+ more bills pending across 13 states.

The structural barrier to a home repair business was never skill — it was parts. Manufacturers restricted genuine components and service documentation to authorized channels, so independents worked from grey-market parts and guesswork. The new state laws require manufacturers to make "parts and repair instructions available to the general public," and the newer bills go further by banning parts pairing (the software lockout that makes a genuine replacement component refuse to work unless the manufacturer blesses it). Combined with the memory and component shortage pushing new-device prices up 10–20% this year, the economics flipped: repairing a three-year-old laptop is now obviously cheaper than replacing it, and consumers know it. This is a service business with essentially no inventory risk if you order parts per-job.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $300–$900 (precision driver set, heat mat, suction tools, spudgers, multimeter, magnetic project mat, starter stock of the 3–4 most common screens and batteries)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 2–3 weeks (including practice repairs on junk units)
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 8–12 hrs/week; most screen and battery jobs run 30–45 minutes once practiced
- 📈 **Return potential:** Shops commonly charge $80–$150 per repair at roughly $50 average profit per job; 6–10 jobs a weekend is a realistic ceiling for one person
- 🔁 **Model:** One-off service revenue, with strong repeat and referral flow
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Steady hands, a clean bench, and accounts at parts distributors (MobileSentrix, Injured Gadgets, Mobile Defenders are the commonly cited US suppliers)
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** Confirm your own state's law — the statutes differ sharply by state and by device category, and several are still pending. Check local rules on operating a service business from home, and decide up front whether you'll touch data-bearing devices (if so, write a simple data-handling policy). Avoid anything battery-swelling or liquid-damaged until you're experienced; lithium cells are the real hazard.
- 🔧 **Skill level:** Beginner-friendly for screens and batteries; board-level work is a different trade
- 🌐 **Local vs online:** Local — this is a geography-bound service, which is exactly why it's defensible

**First steps**
1. Read your state's actual right-to-repair statute this week (start with the Waste Dive roundup, then the bill text). Note precisely which device categories are covered and what manufacturers must supply — that's your legal basis for requesting parts and manuals.
2. Pick **one** device family to start: iPhone 12–14 screens and batteries, or MacBook Air batteries, or Chromebook screens. Depth beats breadth when you have a $900 budget.
3. Buy 3–4 junk units of that model for $20–$40 each and do the repair badly, twice, before you charge anyone. Time yourself on the third.
4. Open accounts with two parts distributors and price the top three failures for your chosen model. Set your price at roughly 2.5–3x parts cost, then sanity-check against the nearest walk-in shop.
5. List on Nextdoor, Craigslist, and your neighborhood Facebook group with a fixed, published price list and a same-day turnaround promise. Published pricing is the single biggest differentiator against "bring it in and we'll see" shops.
6. After each job, ask for a review and hand over two business cards. Referral flow is what turns this from a hobby into $1,500–$3,000 a month of weekend income.

### 3. Creatine for the Couch — A Content-and-Affiliate Play for the 45-to-65 Non-Exerciser

> **A clean 12-week RCT just showed sedentary middle-aged adults gained lean mass on creatine alone — and every existing creatine brand is still marketing to 24-year-olds in a squat rack.**

**Source:** https://www.psypost.org/creatine-improves-muscle-mass-and-cognitive-function-in-older-adults-even-without-exercise/ — PsyPost, Sept. 10, 2026. Covers a study in the *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*.

The trial is unusually well-suited to a marketing story: 64 healthy adults aged 45–65 (average 55, 40 of them women), 10g/day of creatine monohydrate split into two 5g doses, 12 weeks. The no-exercise arm gained lean tissue mass, strength, and muscular endurance, plus favorable movement in blood lipids, HbA1c, and markers of cognitive function and memory — **without a structured training program**. That last clause is the whole opportunity. Creatine's entire cultural positioning is gym-bro supplement; this result speaks to a much larger, much wealthier, almost entirely unaddressed audience of people who are not going to start lifting. The scrappy wedge here is **not** launching a supplement brand — private-label creatine requires 10,000kg minimum orders for custom packaging, which is far outside budget. The wedge is owning the *information layer*: the site, newsletter, and comparison content that this audience searches for, monetized by affiliate commissions on products that already exist.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $150–$600 (domain, hosting, email platform, and a few hundred dollars of well-produced original photography or a decent template)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 6–12 weeks — this is the slowest-to-cash idea in the brief, but the cheapest to start and the most compounding
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 6–8 hrs/week writing and publishing
- 📈 **Return potential:** Slow build; supplement affiliate programs typically pay 5–15%. Realistic path is a few hundred dollars a month by month 6, with the real value in an email list you own.
- 🔁 **Model:** Content + affiliate, with an obvious later upgrade to a paid guide or a private-label product once demand is proven
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Ability to read a study and write clearly for non-experts; no supplement industry experience required
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** **This is the big one.** Do not make disease-treatment claims — that converts your site into an unapproved-drug marketing problem under FDA rules. Describe what the study found, cite it, and stop there. Note the study's real limits honestly: n=64, 12 weeks, industry-adjacent funding is common in this field, and cognitive markers are not the same as clinical cognitive outcomes. Creatine is not appropriate for everyone (kidney conditions in particular) — tell readers to ask their doctor and mean it.
- 🎯 **Audience:** 45–65, sedentary, health-curious, not gym-going — and notably under-served, since 40 of the 64 participants were women and almost no creatine marketing addresses them
- 📊 **Scalability:** High — content assets don't require your presence once ranked

**First steps**
1. Read the actual paper in the *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, not just the PsyPost writeup. Note the exact dose protocol, the population, and every limitation the authors state. Your credibility is the entire product.
2. Buy a domain aimed squarely at the audience (something in the shape of "creatine after 50" / "strength after 50"), and stand up a simple site on a $10/month host. Don't spend money on design yet.
3. Write the five articles this audience is actually searching for: what the study found in plain English; creatine and women over 45; creatine without exercise — what it will and won't do; monohydrate vs. the expensive forms (spoiler: monohydrate won); and what to ask your doctor first.
4. Apply to Amazon Associates and 2–3 direct supplement affiliate programs. Compare 5–6 monohydrate products on price per 5g serving and third-party testing status, and publish that comparison as your cornerstone page — price-per-serving math is genuinely useful and almost nobody publishes it.
5. Put an email capture on every page offering a one-page "getting started" PDF. The list is the asset; the affiliate revenue is just the early proof.
6. Re-evaluate at month 4. If the list is growing and specific products convert, *that's* when a private-label product or a paid guide makes sense — funded by revenue, not by your $2,000.

### 4. The East Bay Rent Compliance Concierge — $397/Unit Fees and 100% Penalties Sell Themselves

> **Berkeley charges $397 per unit per year and doubles it if you're a day late; Oakland bars you from raising rent at all if you missed registration. Small landlords are one spreadsheet away from five-figure mistakes.**

**Sources:** https://alleastbayproperties.com/east-bay-rent-increase-2026/ — All East Bay Properties (updated Sept. 2026), and https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/local/bay-area-voter-guide-2026-election/4126572/ — NBC Bay Area (updated Sept. 29, 2026), for the pending Berkeley Measure X rent-cap change.

The East Bay rent-regulation landscape is a patchwork that changes every year, and the penalties for getting it wrong are asymmetric. The 2026 Annual General Adjustments alone run Oakland 2.3%, Berkeley 1.0%, Richmond 1.5%, while Emeryville has no local cap and falls back to AB 1482's 8.8% — four different numbers within a fifteen-minute drive. Berkeley's registration fees for FY 2026-27 are $397/unit (fully covered) and $244/unit (partially covered), due July 2, with a **100% penalty** for late payment. Oakland's registration renewal deadline was March 2, and owners who missed it cannot file for or impose rent increases, and face failure-to-register as an affirmative defense in most eviction actions. Richmond allows treble damages. Meanwhile Measure X on the November 3 ballot would change Berkeley's rules again. The mom-and-pop owner with two to six units — no property manager, no lawyer on retainer — is carrying all this risk on memory, and is very willing to pay someone to carry it for them.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $200–$800 (business registration, a simple site, scheduling and e-sign tools, and errors-and-omissions insurance quotes)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 2–4 weeks
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 5–8 hrs/week, heavily concentrated around the March and July deadlines
- 📈 **Return potential:** $200–$500 per owner for an annual compliance package; 20–30 clients is a realistic year-one ceiling for nights-and-weekends effort, and renewals are near-automatic
- 🔁 **Model:** Recurring — annual deadlines guarantee the repeat
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Diligence and reading comprehension. No license is required to prepare and file forms or run calculations.
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** **Do not give legal advice or represent clients at rent board hearings** — that's the line between a service business and unauthorized practice of law, and California enforces it. You calculate, prepare, remind, and file; an attorney handles disputes. Say so in writing in your engagement letter. Carry E&O insurance before your first paying client. Rules change annually and Measure X may change Berkeley's again in November — build re-checking the ordinances into your calendar, not your memory.
- 📅 **Deadline calendar:** Oakland registration renewal (early March), Berkeley registration fees (July 2), plus each city's AGA effective date
- 🌐 **Local vs online:** Hyper-local — and that's the moat

**First steps**
1. Spend one weekend reading the primary sources, not blog summaries: Oakland's Rent Adjustment Program registration pages, the Berkeley Rent Board's fee schedule and FY 2026-27 notices, Richmond's rent program, and AB 1482's text. Build a single spreadsheet: city, AGA %, effective window, registration deadline, fee, penalty, notice period.
2. Attend Oakland RAP's free counseling sessions and a Berkeley Rent Board meeting. You will learn the actual failure modes, and you'll meet confused owners in the room.
3. Productize three tiers: **Annual Compliance** (registration filed, fees calendared, AGA calculated) at ~$150/unit; **Rent Increase Package** (correct AGA, compliant notice drafted, correct delivery method and timing) at ~$200 per notice; **Annual Retainer** bundling both plus deadline alerts.
4. Write your engagement letter with an explicit "we are not attorneys and do not provide legal advice" clause before you take a dollar. Get E&O quotes the same week.
5. Find the first ten clients where they already are: Berkeley and Oakland small-landlord Facebook groups, East Bay Rental Housing Association events, and Nextdoor. Lead with the penalty, not the service — "Berkeley's late fee is 100% of what you owe" opens more conversations than "compliance services available."
6. Offer the first three clients a free audit of their current registration status. You will find errors, those errors convert to paid work, and those three become your referral engine.

### 5. Five Weeks to Election Day — Freelance Digital Ops for Local Ballot-Measure Committees

> **Ten-plus Bay Area measures go before voters on November 3, every committee spending over $270 is publicly listed, and most of them have money but no one who can actually run a digital ad account.**

**Source:** https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/local/bay-area-voter-guide-2026-election/4126572/ — NBC Bay Area (published Sept. 17, updated Sept. 29, 2026). Berkeley Measures X (10% rent-increase cap), Y (arts parcel tax), Z (public bank parcel tax) and AA (soda tax); Oakland EE (strong mayor) and FF (foreclosure transfer-tax exemption); regional Measure RTM (five-county transit sales tax); Redwood City Measure E (rent limits); Solano Measure E (data center tax).

Local ballot-measure committees are the most under-resourced buyers of marketing labor in existence. They are typically run by volunteers or a single part-time organizer, they have real money from donors, they are on a hard deadline, and they consistently cannot find anyone to competently run a Meta ad account, build a one-page site, cut short vertical video, or manage a texting tool. The window is short and closes absolutely on November 3 — which is the point: the deadline is what forces a fast yes or no, and there are ten-plus committees within driving distance. The unusual advantage in this niche is that **the buyers' budgets are public record**. California and local disclosure rules require committees to file campaign statements, so you can read exactly who has raised money before you pitch them.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $0–$300 (a portfolio page, Canva, a domain; the ad spend is the client's, not yours)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 1–2 weeks — campaigns on a deadline decide fast and pay fast
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 10–15 hrs/week for the five weeks to Election Day; a genuine sprint
- 📈 **Return potential:** *Estimate, not grounded in disclosed figures* — $1,000–$4,000 per committee for a five-week engagement, with 2–3 committees realistically manageable at once. Verify against actual filings before you price.
- 🔁 **Model:** One-off project work, but every election cycle repeats and local politics is a small, referral-dense world
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Working competence in Meta/Google Ads, basic landing pages, and short-form video. No political experience required — competence is the scarce input.
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** Campaign finance rules apply to *you* as a paid vendor. Committees must file for independent expenditures of $1,000+ within 24 hours during the 8/5–11/3 window; understand what you must document and invoice cleanly. Get paid in advance or in milestones — campaigns are notorious for ending in debt, and after November 3 there is no leverage. Note that Oakland's public financing programs are **not funded** for 2026, so Oakland committees are on donor money only.
- ⏳ **Hard deadline:** November 3, 2026 — roughly five weeks out
- 🔁 **Repeatability:** Every June and November cycle, indefinitely

**First steps**
1. Pull the public filings **today**. Berkeley's campaign finance page and Oakland's campaign filing portal list committees and their disclosed receipts. Rank the ten-plus measures by money raised — that is your qualified lead list, in order.
2. Pick the three best-funded committees whose position you'd genuinely be comfortable working for. This matters practically as well as ethically: you'll be asked why you care, and local politics has a long memory.
3. Before pitching, build one free spec asset for each — a 30-second vertical video or a clean one-page site mockup for their measure. In a five-week sprint, showing beats proposing, and it costs you a Saturday.
4. Email the committee treasurer listed on the filing (public information) with the spec asset attached and a flat five-week price. Treasurers and campaign managers are the decision-makers; volunteers are not.
5. Structure every engagement as 50% up front, 50% at the halfway mark, with the client holding the ad account and paying the platform directly. Never front ad spend.
6. On November 4, ask each client for a written testimonial and an introduction to two other committees. That's what converts a five-week sprint into a business that reappears every cycle.
