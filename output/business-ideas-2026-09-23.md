### 1. The GLP-1 Off-Ramp Desk

> **A third of a million veterans just proved that quitting Ozempic is a cardiac event waiting to happen — build the service that keeps people from quitting over a denied claim.**

**Source:** https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260920222414.htm — ScienceDaily (Sept 21, 2026), covering a *BMJ Medicine* study. Among 333,687 U.S. veterans with type 2 diabetes (132,551 on GLP-1 drugs), stopping the drug raised major cardiovascular event risk 14% at one year and 22% at two years; restarting restored only partial protection.

The study's own recommendation is unusually operational for a journal paper: health systems should "address cost barriers and identify patients at risk of discontinuation." Cost and side effects are what push people off these drugs, and both are administrative problems long before they are medical ones — a denied prior authorization, an unclaimed manufacturer copay card, a formulary change nobody explained. Nobody is paid to fix that for the patient. A solo operator with good templates and patience on hold can be.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $600–$1,400 (LLC + registered agent ~$200, website + scheduling ~$150, e-fax/HIPAA-conscious intake ~$30/mo, initial ads ~$400)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 2–4 weeks
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 6–10 hrs/week; each appeal packet is ~90 min once templated
- 📈 **Return potential:** $150–$300 per appeal packet, or $19–$29/mo continuity membership *(pricing is my estimate, not sourced)*
- 🔁 **Model:** One-off fee with recurring upsell
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Strong writing, a library of payer appeal templates, familiarity with manufacturer patient-assistance programs
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** Do **not** give medical advice or promise outcomes. You are an administrative advocate. Avoid handling PHI loosely; use a BAA-capable intake tool. Some states regulate paid patient advocacy — check yours.
- ⚡ **Speed to cash:** High — patients facing a January formulary reset will pay this week

**First steps**
1. Pick one payer and one drug to start (e.g. the largest commercial plan in your metro + one GLP-1). Read its published prior-authorization and appeal criteria end to end, and turn the criteria into a checklist.
2. Draft three reusable letters — first-level appeal, peer-to-peer request, and formulary-exception request — with bracketed fields for the patient's specifics. Cite the *BMJ Medicine* discontinuation finding in the medical-necessity section; "stopping raises 2-year MACE risk 22%" is a genuinely strong argument on paper.
3. Build a one-page site: what you do, what you don't do (no medical advice), flat price, turnaround time.
4. Do five cases free in GLP-1 patient forums and Reddit communities in exchange for a written outcome and a testimonial. Track your win rate honestly.
5. Once you have three wins, run $300 of narrowly targeted ads ("GLP-1 denied? Appeal packet in 72 hours") and price at $199.
6. Add the $29/mo tier: annual re-authorization calendar reminders, copay-card re-enrollment, and formulary-change alerts. That is the recurring revenue.

### 2. Rebate Recon

> **Lake Powell just set an all-time low, Southwest water agencies are paying homeowners by the square foot to rip out grass, and almost nobody wants to fill out the form.**

**Source:** https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260922005701.htm — ScienceDaily (Sept 22, 2026). Lake Powell fell to 3,517.24 feet on Sept 10, 2026, below the previous record of 3,519.92 set in April 2023, after a severe 2025–26 snow drought. Over 40 million people and ~5 million acres of farmland depend on the system; federal officials have already cut Powell-to-Mead releases and canceled environmental flows.

Record lows are what move water agencies from encouraging conservation to paying for it. Turf-replacement and irrigation-retrofit rebate programs across Las Vegas, Phoenix, Southern California and the Inland Empire pay per square foot — but they require before-and-after photos, accurate measurements, plant lists, and inspection scheduling, which is exactly why participation lags. The low-capital wedge is not landscaping (capital-heavy, licensed). It is being the person who measures, photographs, files, and shepherds the rebate.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $400–$900 (measuring wheel ~$40, 100-ft tape ~$20, phone tripod ~$30, simple site ~$150, business cards/door hangers ~$200, mileage)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 2–3 weeks
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 8–12 hrs/week; a single-family measure-and-file is ~2 hrs
- 📈 **Return potential:** Flat $250–$450 per home filing, or 15–20% of rebate awarded; HOA and small-commercial jobs are multiples of that *(pricing is my estimate)*
- 🔁 **Model:** One-off service with referral flywheel
- 🌐 **Where:** Colorado River service areas — Las Vegas, Phoenix/Scottsdale, Southern California, not the Bay Area
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Measuring tools, a phone camera, meticulousness, and one afternoon reading your local agency's rebate rulebook
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** Do not perform the removal work itself without a contractor's license — stay on the documentation side. Rebate program rules and funding caps change mid-year; confirm remaining budget before selling.
- 🔧 **Skill level:** Low — this is diligence, not expertise

**First steps**
1. Choose one agency (Southern Nevada Water Authority, a Phoenix-area provider, or a SoCal district) and read its current turf-rebate rules cover to cover: per-square-foot rate, remaining funding, pre-inspection requirements, approved plant list, photo specs.
2. Do your own house or a friend's as case zero. Measure, photograph, file, and follow it through inspection. Document every place the process is confusing — that list is your marketing copy.
3. Build a two-page site with the agency's current rate, your flat fee, and a "you keep the rebate, I keep the fee" promise.
4. Partner rather than compete: bring your service to two local landscapers and one HOA management company as a white-label add-on. They have the volume; you have the patience for forms.
5. Door-hang 200 homes on streets with visibly large lawns in your target district. Lead with the dollar figure the agency pays, not with drought.
6. Once you have five completed rebates, quote an HOA common-area project. One of those is worth a month of single-family work.

### 3. The 338 Memo

> **The SBA is about to redraw the line between "small" and "not small" for every federal contractor in America — and 114,500 firms are about to need a straight answer, fast.**

**Source:** https://www.greaterrochesterchamber.com/2026/09/08/sba-proposed-rule-change-heres-what-you-need-to-know/ — Greater Rochester Chamber of Commerce (Sept 8, 2026). SBA proposed rule (Docket SBA-2026-0199) would consolidate nearly 1,000 six-digit NAICS size standards into 338 broader four- and five-digit standards, shift the default measure from annual receipts to employee counts in most industries, add a productivity-growth adjustment, and bring roughly 114,500 additional firms into the small-business pool. Public comment closed Sept 21, 2026; current standards stay in effect until a final rule issues.

This is the most profitable kind of news for a solo operator: a rule that changes a binary status thousands of businesses have organized their revenue around, with a gap between proposal and final rule in which everybody wants clarity and nobody wants to pay a law firm's retainer for it. Two distinct customers appear at once — incumbents who may lose small status or face 114,500 new bidders, and newly-eligible firms who have never registered in SAM.gov and don't know where to start.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $300–$800 (LLC ~$200, site ~$150, LinkedIn Sales Navigator ~$100/mo optional). The primary sources — the Federal Register text, SAM.gov, USASpending.gov, SBA's size-standards tables — are all free.
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 3–5 weeks
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 10 hrs/week; each memo is ~4–6 hrs until you have a template
- 📈 **Return potential:** $500–$1,500 per impact memo; $750–$1,500 per SAM.gov + capability-statement onboarding *(pricing is my estimate)*
- 🔁 **Model:** Productized one-off, with a natural annual-recertification renewal
- ⏳ **Timing window:** Now through final rule — urgency decays sharply after it issues
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Comfort with the Federal Register, NAICS tables, SAM.gov, and USASpending.gov; clean document design
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** This is research and administrative work, **not** legal advice — say so in writing on every deliverable. Size determination and affiliation rules are genuinely tricky; refer affiliation questions to a government-contracts attorney rather than guessing.

**First steps**
1. Read the proposed rule in the Federal Register (Docket SBA-2026-0199) in full, plus the public comments now on the docket — the comments are a free, verbatim list of which industries are most upset, which is your target list.
2. Build a one-page "before/after" lookup: for each industry you cover, the current six-digit standard versus the proposed four/five-digit standard and measure (receipts vs. employees). This spreadsheet *is* the product.
3. Write one free impact memo for a real firm — pull a small contractor from USASpending.gov, do the analysis, and send it cold with no ask. Use it as your sample.
4. Pick two or three NAICS families you'll specialize in (janitorial, IT services, construction trades are high-volume set-aside categories) and make the memo template reusable.
5. Sell through channels, not cold outreach: PTACs/APEX Accelerators, chambers of commerce, and local SBA resource partners all field these questions and have no one to hand them to. Offer to give a free 30-minute webinar to their members.
6. Add the second product for the newly-eligible 114,500: a fixed-price SAM.gov registration + capability statement + first-three-bids package.

### 4. Assessment Appeal Co.

> **Consumer sentiment just fell to 47.8 while housing starts and permits both declined — which means a lot of homes are assessed at a value they no longer have.**

**Source:** https://www.thornhillfinancialservices.net/market-commentary/market-commentary-september-21st-2026/ — Thornhill Financial Services, Market Commentary (Sept 21, 2026). The Fed raised the target range 25bp to 3.75%–4.00%; the 10-year Treasury sits near 5.0% and the 30-year near 5.34%; housing starts fell 2.6% to a 1.275M annualized pace with permits down 2.7%; University of Michigan sentiment dropped to 47.8 from 51.7 with year-ahead inflation expectations at 4.6%. Retail sales nonetheless rose 1.2% in August.

The interesting tension: households are still spending but feel terrible, and financing costs are grinding on housing. Assessed values are set on a lag, so in softening submarkets a meaningful share of homeowners are paying tax on a number the market no longer supports. Appeals are free or nearly free to file, decided on comparable sales, and almost nobody files them — which makes contingency-fee representation a clean, near-zero-capital business. It is also the rare service a value-seeking, low-confidence household says yes to instantly, because it costs them nothing unless it works.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $200–$900 (LLC ~$200, site ~$150, optional comparable-sales data ~$50–$200/mo; county assessor records are free)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** Depends entirely on your county's appeal window — cash lands after a decision, so 1–6 months
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 8–12 hrs/week during appeal season, near zero outside it
- 📈 **Return potential:** 25–50% of first-year tax savings is the common contingency structure; a $700 annual saving yields $175–$350 per home, and the work is highly templated *(structure is typical industry practice; treat the specific percentages as speculation)*
- 🔁 **Model:** Contingency one-off, seasonal and repeatable annually
- 📊 **Scalability:** High — comp research templates once, then volume is limited only by filings
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Assessor portal fluency, comparable-sales analysis, a clear one-page engagement agreement
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** **This is the make-or-break item.** Many states restrict who may represent a property owner in a tax appeal to attorneys, licensed agents, or the owner themselves, and some regulate contingency fees. Confirm your state's rules *before* spending a dollar. Where representation is restricted, sell a flat-fee "appeal evidence packet" the owner files themselves instead.
- 🗓️ **Seasonality:** Appeal deadlines are fixed and unforgiving — build the calendar first

**First steps**
1. Before anything else, look up your state's rules on non-attorney representation in property tax appeals and on contingency fees. This single answer determines whether you sell representation or sell a packet.
2. Pull your own county's assessment roll and appeal calendar. Note the filing deadline — it is the whole business's clock.
3. Run the analysis on 20 homes in one neighborhood: assessed value vs. the last 6–12 months of comparable sales. Count how many are over-assessed by more than 10%. That hit rate tells you whether your county is worth working.
4. Appeal your own property, or a friend's, start to finish. You need one completed case before you sell.
5. Write a two-page engagement agreement: contingency percentage (or flat packet fee), what you do, what the owner does, and no guarantee of outcome.
6. Market to the over-assessed list you built in step 3 with a letter showing *their* assessment against *their* neighbors' recent sales. Specificity converts; generic mailers do not.

### 5. Ingredient Cost Watch

> **A 50-pound sack of yellow onions went from $12.95 to $27.95 in twelve months — independent restaurants are absorbing that blind, and repricing a menu properly is a weekend's work.**

**Source:** https://oaklandside.org/2026/09/17/cafe-colucci-vegan-combo-restaurant-economics-tariffs/ — The Oaklandside (Sept 17, 2026). Oakland's Cafe Colucci faces a 115% increase in yellow onions ($12.95 → $27.95 per 50-lb sack, June 2025–June 2026), green leaf lettuce up from $16.95 to $27.95 per case, berbere spice up 5.5%, and gas up over 30% since February 2026 — against imported Ethiopian and Indian staples caught in shifting tariffs. Owner Daniel Yeshiwas describes "unprecedented price volatility," raised the vegan combo only 7% (to $21) since January 2025, and filed a tariff refund claim in May 2026 that is still unanswered.

The story is a portrait of an operator flying blind: aggregating price changes by feel, holding prices to protect the dish's "DNA," and waiting on a refund. What's missing is per-dish cost accounting updated against actual invoices — the thing every restaurant knows it should do and almost no independent has time for. That is a purely informational product, which is why a solo operator can sell it for real money with no inventory and no kitchen.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $150–$600 (spreadsheet or Airtable ~$25/mo, simple site ~$150, printing for sample reports ~$100)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 2–4 weeks — restaurants decide fast and pay on completion
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 10 hrs/week; first engagement ~15 hrs, later ones ~6 once templated
- 📈 **Return potential:** $600–$900 per initial costing engagement + $99–$149/mo monitoring *(pricing is my estimate)*
- 🔁 **Model:** One-off engagement converting to recurring monitoring
- 🎯 **Target customer:** Independent, owner-operated, ingredient-import-dependent restaurants — Ethiopian, Indian, Thai, Mexican; the East Bay has hundreds
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Spreadsheet fluency, ability to read distributor invoices, enough tact to tell an owner their signature dish loses money
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** **Tariff refunds belong to the importer of record** — usually the distributor (Brundo, Milan International), not the restaurant. Do not sell restaurants a refund service they have no standing to claim; if you want that line of business, sell it to the small importers instead. Restaurants are cash-poor, so price the first engagement low and make money on monitoring.
- ⚡ **Speed to cash:** High — an owner watching onions double will take a meeting this week

**First steps**
1. Pick one cuisine with heavy imported inputs and walk ten restaurants in it. Ask one question: "How much has your biggest ingredient gone up this year, and when did you last reprice?" The answers will be vague — that vagueness is the market.
2. Build the costing template: per-dish ingredient list, current unit cost, yield/waste factor, plate cost, current menu price, contribution margin, and a break-even reprice figure.
3. Do one restaurant free, with their last 12 months of invoices, in exchange for permission to use it as an anonymized case study. Deliver a 3-page report, not a spreadsheet dump — the report is the product.
4. Add the part they cannot do themselves: a monthly watchlist of their top 15 ingredients with price movement and a flag when any dish crosses below target margin.
5. Sell at $650 for the initial costing plus $129/mo monitoring. Pitch it as "one repriced dish pays for this," which for a $21 combo at volume is simply true.
6. Once you have five clients in one cuisine, you are holding comparative pricing data nobody else has — that is when a group-buy coordination service or an anonymized regional price index becomes a second, higher-margin product.
