### 1. The Data Act Readiness Audit
> **A hard EU deadline lands September 12 and the small hardware companies it hits have no compliance department — sell them a fixed-fee, one-week readiness audit.**

**Source:** https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2026/06/eu-data-act-september-2026-deadline-what-businesses-need-to-know/ — Wilson Sonsini (The Data Advisor). Explains that from 12 Sept 2026, connected products placed on the EU market must be built with "access-by-design" data functionality.

From 12 September 2026, anything connected sold into the EU must make its product and related-service data "easily, securely and directly accessible to users, free of charge" where technically feasible, in a structured, machine-readable format — plus updated pre-contractual disclosures and user terms. Big manufacturers have counsel on this. The long tail does not: e-bike and scooter brands, ag and environmental sensor makers, smart-appliance importers, dev-board and industrial-IoT shops, EV charger vendors. The work — inventory the product line, map which data qualifies, judge technical feasibility, rewrite the terms — is checklist work, not litigation, and it is repeatable across clients once you have built the checklist once.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $300–$900 (domain, LLC/sole-trader setup, the official Regulation text is free)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 2–5 weeks
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 8–12 hrs/week; each audit is roughly 10–15 hrs of work
- 📈 **Return potential:** $1,500–$4,000 per audit; 2–3/month is realistic for a solo operator with a technical background
- 🔁 **Model:** Productized one-off service, with a recurring annual re-check upsell
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Technical literacy in APIs/embedded data, careful reading, a clean checklist doc and a report template
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** **You are not a law firm — sell this as a technical readiness assessment, not legal advice**, and say so in writing. Partner with or refer to an actual EU-qualified lawyer for the contract language. Deadline-driven demand decays after the date passes, so the durable version is annual re-checks and new-product reviews.
- 🎯 **Sweet-spot client:** 10–80 person hardware companies with EU distribution and no in-house counsel
- ⚡ **Speed to cash:** High — the deadline does the selling for you

**First steps**
1. Read the Data Act's Chapter II access obligations directly (EUR-Lex, free) and build a 25–40 line audit checklist: data inventory, direct-vs-indirect access, format, documentation, contract terms.
2. Run the checklist against one real product you can inspect publicly — a popular e-bike or smart sensor — and write the output as a sample report. This is your entire sales asset.
3. Build a one-page site: the deadline, the three obligations, your fixed fee, and the sample report behind an email gate.
4. Pull a list of 150–300 small connected-product companies selling into the EU (Amazon EU listings, Kickstarter hardware graduates, trade-show exhibitor lists) and email 30/week with a two-line pitch plus the sample report.
5. Price the first three at $1,200 to buy testimonials, then move to $2,500–$3,500. Offer a free 20-minute scoping call — it closes this category well.
6. After the deadline passes, pivot the same checklist into an annual re-certification and a new-product pre-launch review.

### 2. The AI Hiring Paper Trail Kit
> **Every US employer running résumé-screening software now owes applicants notices and records under five different state laws — sell the templated compliance pack, not the $5,500 statistical audit.**

**Source:** https://synhr.com/ai-hiring-regulations/ — SynHR. Maps the 2026–2027 patchwork: Illinois HB 3773 (live 1 Jan 2026), New Jersey disparate-impact rules (in force), California ADMT (1 Jan 2027), Colorado AI Act (1 Jan 2027), Connecticut (Oct 2027).

Regulators moved from writing rules to checking them: reporting this month has the EU AI Office and 24 national authorities opening their first inspection wave, with automated résumé screening named as a priority sector. In the US the obligations are already live in Illinois and New Jersey. But note what employers actually owe: pre-use applicant notices, an inventory of every screening and ranking tool, vendor due-diligence records, documented human-review checkpoints, and retained impact assessments. That is document work. The **statistical bias audit is a different product** — it requires an independent auditor with I/O psychology or statistics credentials and sells for around $5,500 a tool — so don't compete there. Sell the layer underneath it, which every employer needs and nobody sells cheaply.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $400–$1,200 (site, document tooling, a few hours of a lawyer's time to review your templates — worth paying for)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 3–6 weeks
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 6–10 hrs/week
- 📈 **Return potential:** $600–$1,500 per templated pack; $2,000–$4,000 for done-for-you implementation at a mid-size employer
- 🔁 **Model:** Digital product with a services upsell; renews as each state's date arrives
- 🧰 **Need to start:** HR/recruiting-ops familiarity, clear writing, and the discipline to track five statutes as they change
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** **Templates are not legal advice** — disclaim it plainly and have a licensed employment attorney review the pack before you sell it. Do not market this as a bias audit or imply independence you don't have. Statutes are being amended; a stale template is a liability.
- 📊 **Scalability:** High — the same pack resells with per-state deltas
- 🎯 **Sweet-spot client:** 50–500 employee companies using an ATS with AI ranking and no dedicated employment counsel

**First steps**
1. Build a one-page matrix of the five regimes: who's covered, effective date, notice required, records required. Give it away free — it is the lead magnet and it establishes competence immediately.
2. Draft the pack: applicant pre-use notice (per state), AEDT/ADMT tool inventory sheet, vendor due-diligence questionnaire, human-review checkpoint policy, records-retention schedule.
3. Pay an employment attorney for 2–3 hours to review and mark up the templates. Budget $400–$800. Do not skip this.
4. Post the free matrix in HR-ops communities (r/humanresources, HR Open Source, local SHRM chapters) and on LinkedIn, with the pack as the paid follow-on.
5. Sell the pack at $499 self-serve. Offer a $1,500 "we fill it in with you" session — most buyers take it, and it's two hours of work.
6. Build a referral loop with an independent bias-audit firm: you do the paperwork layer, they do the statistics, you send each other work.

### 3. Fuel Surcharge Overbilling Recovery
> **Diesel is up 63% in a year, surcharges on every freight invoice have quietly ballooned, and almost no small shipper is checking the arithmetic — audit their invoices for a cut of what you find back.**

**Source:** https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/us-diesel-price-supply-chain-shock-september-2026/ — Discovery Alert. US diesel hit $6.05/gal on 11 Sept 2026, up 63% from $3.70 a year earlier, driven by Strait of Hormuz disruption cutting up to 14M bbl/day of transit.

Fuel surcharges are formulaic: (current DOE diesel price − base price) ÷ assumed MPG, with the DOE/EIA price published free every week. The formula is so commoditized that half a dozen free calculators exist — which is exactly why *building another calculator is not the business*. The business is that when diesel doubles, the surcharge line on an invoice goes from a rounding error to a large number, base rates and assumed MPG in old contracts drift out of line with what's actually billed, and a small perishable-food distributor or regional retailer paying dozens of freight invoices a month has nobody checking any of it. Fuel is an estimated 15–30% of total food costs; the errors are now material. You work on contingency, so the client's downside is zero and the sale is easy.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $0–$300 (spreadsheet, free EIA data, a simple engagement letter)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 3–8 weeks (recovery takes a billing cycle or two to land)
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 6–10 hrs/week
- 📈 **Return potential:** 25–35% contingency on recovered amounts; a mid-size shipper's annual surcharge spend runs five to six figures, so single findings can be $2k–$15k
- 🔁 **Model:** Contingency-fee arbitrage, converting to a monthly invoice-audit retainer
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Spreadsheet fluency, patience with invoices, ability to read a freight contract's FSC clause
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** You need the client's contracts and invoices — expect an NDA. Recovery depends on the carrier honoring the correction; frame the pitch as "audit + renegotiation," not guaranteed refunds. Contingency work means uneven cash early on. Verify each contract's own base/MPG terms rather than assuming a standard table.
- ⚡ **Speed to cash:** Medium — fast to find errors, slower to collect
- 🎯 **Sweet-spot client:** Regional food distributors, produce wholesalers, and multi-location restaurant groups shipping refrigerated freight

**First steps**
1. Learn the standard FSC tables cold (DOE-classic, step matrix, flat percent) and bookmark the EIA weekly diesel series. Build a spreadsheet that recomputes any invoice's correct surcharge from contract base + MPG.
2. Offer three local businesses a **free** audit of one month of freight invoices. Perishable-food distributors first — they're the most exposed.
3. Write up whatever you find as a one-page finding: billed vs. contractual, the delta, and the annualized number. The annualized figure is what sells the retainer.
4. Convert to a contingency engagement at 30% of recovered/avoided spend for 12 months, with a simple engagement letter.
5. Use the first completed recovery as a case study and cold-email 20 similar shippers a week.
6. Add the adjacent upsell once you have three clients: renegotiating the FSC base and MPG assumptions at contract renewal — that's recurring savings, and worth a flat fee.

### 4. The Evidence-First Knee Brace Desk
> **A 139-trial, 10,000-patient analysis just put knee braces at the top for osteoarthritis pain — build the honest buying guide in a category drowning in junk affiliate content.**

**Source:** https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260913082208.htm — ScienceDaily. A network meta-analysis of 12 non-drug therapies across 139 trials and nearly 10,000 patients ranked knee braces, water-based therapy, and exercise highest for knee osteoarthritis; ultrasound ranked near the bottom.

Knee OA is a mass-market condition with an older, motivated, purchase-ready audience, and the search results for "best knee brace" are almost entirely thin affiliate spam with no reference to evidence. A fresh, large, citable meta-analysis is a genuine content wedge: you can build the one guide that actually maps the evidence to specific brace types (unloader vs. hinged vs. sleeve), explains fit, and says plainly when a brace *isn't* the answer. Monetize through affiliate commissions and, optionally, a paid one-on-one selection consult — but this only works if you are scrupulous about not practicing medicine.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $200–$800 (domain, hosting, two or three braces bought for hands-on photos)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 8–16 weeks — **slowest of the five; SEO has a lag**
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 6–10 hrs/week
- 📈 **Return potential:** Speculative. Health affiliate niches can reach $500–$3,000/month within a year, but this depends entirely on ranking and is the least certain payoff in this brief.
- 🔁 **Model:** Content + affiliate, with an optional paid consult
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Clear writing, ability to read a meta-analysis accurately, basic SEO, a camera
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** **This is the legally touchiest idea here.** Do not diagnose, do not promise outcomes, and route readers to clinicians. FTC rules require conspicuous affiliate disclosure. Braces sold for medical purposes carry FDA labeling considerations — you are reviewing, not manufacturing or making device claims. If you offer paid consults, be explicit that you are not a medical professional (or partner with a licensed PT and split the fee).
- 🌐 **Online vs local:** Fully online; the paid-consult version works better with a local PT partner
- 🔧 **Skill level:** Low technical, high diligence

**First steps**
1. Read the actual paper, not the press release — get the journal citation from ScienceDaily and pull the network meta-analysis so you can cite effect sizes and, honestly, its limitations.
2. Write the cornerstone piece: "What the 2026 meta-analysis actually found about knee braces," including where braces *didn't* win and where exercise or aquatic therapy beat them. The honesty is the differentiator.
3. Join Amazon Associates plus one or two direct brace-brand affiliate programs (they typically pay better than Amazon).
4. Build out 8–12 supporting pages on long-tail queries: unloader vs. hinged, bracing for medial compartment OA, what to ask a physio, sizing and fit.
5. Seed distribution where the audience actually is — arthritis forums, running and cycling communities, Facebook groups for over-55s — contributing genuinely rather than dropping links.
6. Once traffic is steady, test a $75 30-minute "which brace and why" consult, ideally co-delivered with a licensed physical therapist.

### 5. Closing-Kitchen Broker
> **East Bay restaurants keep closing and their owners get lowballed on equipment by dealers — broker the sale to the next operator for a commission and never buy a single walk-in cooler.**

**Source:** https://www.berkeleyside.org/2026/09/11/eggys-ristorante-due-rose-sweet-potatoes-up-beet-closed — Berkeleyside. Reports another round of East Bay restaurant closures and relocations (Sweet Potatoes Up Beet Cafe shutting down, Eggy's relocating), part of an elevated closure rate across the East Bay. *(Note: the article page returned an HTTP 429 on direct fetch; details confirmed via Berkeleyside's homepage listing.)*

Every closure is a distressed seller with a walk-in, a hood, a combi oven and a three-comp sink they need gone before the lease ends — and a strong incentive to accept a dealer's lowball cash offer because it's fast. Meanwhile every new operator in the same corridor wants exactly that gear at half of new. The obvious business here is buying and reselling equipment, which needs a truck, a warehouse and real cash. **The low-capital wedge is brokering:** you never take title, never store anything, and charge a commission for matching the closing kitchen to the opening one and coordinating the pickup. The East Bay's density of closures and openings inside a few square miles is what makes a solo operator viable.

**Snapshot**
- 💵 **Initial investment:** $150–$600 (phone, good camera, listing fees, business cards; **no inventory, no truck**)
- ⏱️ **Time to first revenue:** 2–6 weeks — closures move fast when a lease is ending
- 🗓️ **Ongoing effort:** 8–12 hrs/week, concentrated in evenings and weekends when restaurant owners are actually reachable
- 📈 **Return potential:** 10–20% commission on a $5k–$40k equipment package; one deal a month is a solid side income
- 🔁 **Model:** Brokerage / matchmaking commission
- 🧰 **Need to start:** Local legwork, a phone, decent photography, and a working sense of what used commercial gear is worth
- ⚖️ **Watch-outs:** You are brokering **equipment**, not businesses or leases — brokering the sale of a business or a lease assignment in California can require a real estate or business-opportunity broker license, so keep your scope to equipment and refer the lease work out. Get a signed one-page consignment agreement before you list anything. Landlords sometimes claim fixtures; confirm who actually owns the hood and the walk-in before promising it.
- 🌐 **Online vs local:** Local sourcing, regional demand (Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, restaurant groups)
- ⚡ **Speed to cash:** High — distressed timelines create urgency on both sides

**First steps**
1. Spend one weekend learning comps: browse SF Bay Craigslist and the local auction houses (American Restaurant Sales runs biweekly Bay Area auctions) and build a price sheet for the 20 most common items — hoods, walk-ins, ranges, combi ovens, prep tables, three-comp sinks.
2. Track closures weekly via Berkeleyside's Nosh and Oaklandside's Nosh, plus ABC license transfers and health-permit closures. This list *is* your pipeline.
3. Approach 5–10 closing or recently closed restaurants directly and offer the pitch plainly: *"A dealer will offer you 20 cents on the dollar and I'll get you 50 — you pay me only out of what sells."*
4. Draft a one-page consignment agreement: commission rate, timeline, who arranges pickup, what happens to unsold items.
5. Build the demand side in parallel — email and visit every restaurant under construction or with a pending health permit in Berkeley, Oakland and Emeryville, and offer a free "what are you still missing" list.
6. Do the first deal at 10% to prove it works, then raise to 15–20%. Once you have repeat buyers, a simple email list of opening operators becomes the real asset.
